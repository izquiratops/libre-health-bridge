Executive summary

This work adds an optional, one-time-purchase Premium feature for best-effort automatic LibreLinkUp-to-Apple Health syncing in the background. Manual sync remains free. StoreKit 2 handles purchase and ownership; iOS BackgroundTasks requests future opportunities to sync. The feature shares the existing import path and completion timestamp rather than introducing a second importer or a separate sync history.

Automatic execution remains subject to iOS scheduling and data-protection constraints. The selected 15-, 30-, or 60-minute interval is an earliest requested delay—not a promise that an import will occur on that schedule.

User-facing features

  • Premium purchase and restore. StoreKit 2 non-consumable product com.jordi.librehealthbridge.premium, localized price, purchase and restore actions, verified entitlement checks, and transaction-update observation. Buying Premium does not automatically turn on syncing.
  • Automatic-sync settings. A new Home-screen entry opens a dedicated settings screen. Premium owners can opt in and choose 15, 30, or 60 minutes (30 minutes by default). Manual sync remains available without Premium.
  • Clear limitations. English and Spanish disclosures explain that iOS controls background execution, a locked phone may defer imports (including overnight), and force quit or system settings can prevent attempts. The app is identified as unofficial and not an alarm or treatment-monitoring service.
  • Status and setup gating. The UI reports off, enabled, deferred, unavailable Background App Refresh, or attention/setup states. Enabling requires verified ownership and an eligible, confirmed health-sync setup.

Implementation overview

  • LibreHealthBridge/AutomaticSync.swift implements StoreKit ownership and purchase behavior, the BackgroundTasks scheduler adapter, task registration/completion, and automatic-sync orchestration.
  • AppModel receives injectable premium, scheduler, clock, and device-state dependencies, creating test seams for system-owned behavior and time.
  • Each scheduled attempt rechecks saved state, entitlement, protected-data availability, profile selection/verification, consent, Health access, pause state, and the requested earliest time. It then uses the existing fresh cloud-fetch, normalization/planning, and Health-write flow.
  • Manual and automatic operations share the same operation guard and lastSync completion state. Successful manual sync moves the next eligible background attempt out by the selected interval. Rate limiting can honor the server's Retry-After date; other failures use a conservative retry delay.
  • Opt-in, interval, next eligible time, backoff, and attention/deferred flags are persisted with the existing session. Automatic sync defaults off for existing sessions. Logout, profile changes, and stop/setup transitions clear consent and reconcile pending work.
  • Cold background launch is kept separate from foreground preview restoration. A callback delivered while the app is active does not import data.

Supporting changes

Limitations and follow-up checks

  1. iOS may delay or omit background refresh. A simulator callback or requested interval cannot prove real-world scheduling cadence.
  2. Locked-device data protection can defer work. The feature does not promise overnight syncing or automatic foreground catch-up.
  3. Confirm product ID, price, availability, and review setup in App Store Connect. The local StoreKit configuration is for testing, not proof of a live product.
  4. Run bounded iOS simulator build/model/UI tests, then validate StoreKit restore and revocation in Apple's supported test environment.
  5. Test cold background launch, locked-phone deferral, Background App Refresh disabled, force quit, and interrupted Health saves on a dedicated device.
